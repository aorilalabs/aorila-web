Why per-minute billing

Cloud compute bills by the hour. If your job runs for seven minutes, you pay for sixty. That rounding error is a business model — just not yours.

The minute is the honest unit. You used twelve minutes of a GPU, you pay for twelve minutes. Nothing rounded up, nothing padded.

We keep the math simple on purpose: 1 credit = $1. No conversion tables, no tiers, no fine print to decode before you can estimate a run.

This is how all Aorila Labs compute is billed — per minute, one credit to the dollar, from day one.

The trust moat

Trust and security are number one at Aorila. Not the marketing line — the product itself.

Trust is built out of verifiable facts. Per-minute billing you can check. One credit equals one dollar, always. Workloads that stop at zero instead of billing you into a surprise. Nothing hidden, nothing to reverse-engineer from an invoice.

When you run your work on someone else's machines, you are handing them your code, your data, and your wallet. The only honest way to run that business is to make every rule visible before you start.

That is the moat. Anyone can sell cheap compute. Selling compute you can actually trust is the harder thing to build — so that is what we build.

September 24, 2026

Compute that stops at zero

Here is the rule: your workload stops when your balance hits $0. Not $0 plus a surprise invoice. Zero.

No overages, no end-of-month shock, no job that kept running because nobody told it to quit. You set the ceiling by putting credits in; we enforce it by stopping the machine when they run out.

That means you can start a long run, close the laptop, and sleep. The worst case is bounded: whatever you put in is the most you can spend.

Billing should be a budget tool, not a trap. Ours is.
